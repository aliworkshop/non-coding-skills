# Polish Framework

How to turn someone's raw words (a reply, a form answer, a review, a chat message) into a
pull-quote. The rule underneath all of it: the polished version must still be recognisably
**their** thought, just tightened. If it sounds like a brand wrote it, it's overcooked.

## Three variants

When polishing a fresh response, produce three:

1. **Full arc** — Before → After → Impact, 1-3 sentences. For case studies and proposals.
2. **Punchline** — 1 sentence, the single sharpest line. For a landing page hero, a store
   listing, a social post.
3. **Surface-shaped** — fitted to where it will go (a venue owner's quote for the business
   onboarding page, a developer's quote for a library README, a user's quote for the app
   listing).

Each variant pulls its beats from what they actually said, in their order where possible.

## Voice rules

- **Maximum verbatim.** Stitch their words; don't paraphrase. Drop a dangling "and", cut
  "actually" and "I have to say". If the output uses different words than the source, pull back.
  No invented metaphors, no clichés ("game-changer", "next level"), no claims they didn't make.
- **No em dashes** in the polished output (verbatim raw text keeps whatever it had).
- **Lead with the concrete win when there is one.** "Check-in at the door takes seconds now"
  beats "it made things easier." A number beats an adjective.
- **Each number keeps its own claim shape.** A one-time win and a recurring saving are
  different claims; "on track to save" is not "saved". Don't merge or upgrade them.
- **No self-promotional intensifiers** ("just one call", "only took"). The fact does the work.
- **Vary openers** across quotes that will sit side by side. Check the live ones first.
- **One beat per idea.** Don't preview in sentence 2 what sentence 3 lands.
- **Persian responses stay Persian.** Polish in Persian with the same rules (ZWNJ, punctuation,
  register per `bilingual-copy`). If an English version is also needed, translate the approved
  Persian quote and get approval for that too.

## Permission levels

| Permission | Attribution |
|---|---|
| No | Don't use it. Card → `Declined`. |
| Anonymous | Role + broad descriptor: `— Venue owner, Tehran` or `— Backend lead, mid-size fintech`. Broad enough that it doesn't identify them. |
| First name | `— Sara` |
| Full name | `— Sara Ahmadi` |
| Name + role + company | `— Sara Ahmadi, Owner, Cafe Nur` |
| + photo | Same, plus the image. Flag that Ali needs to get the image. |

When permission is ambiguous, use the smaller level and confirm the exact attribution in the
approval ask. Better to under-share.

**Store reviews** are public under the reviewer's store name. Quoting one on a site or deck
still deserves an ask if the person can be reached; otherwise attribute as
`— App Store review` and quote it exactly, unedited.

## Pre-drafted quotes

When there's no response to polish and Ali offers to pre-draft one: Before → After → Lasting
impact, about 3 sentences, anchored on the single clearest win the person actually described
(in an email, a chat, a call Ali has notes on). Never fill a half they didn't state. If the
gain is a new capability rather than time saved, close on the capability.

## Illustrative example

The names and words below are invented to show the shape; they aren't a real client.

**Raw reply:** "honestly before this we were doing the guest list on paper and a WhatsApp group,
it was a mess every friday. now people just show the code at the door and we see who's coming
before the night starts. would 100% recommend to other bars"

1. **Full arc:** "We used to run the guest list on paper and a WhatsApp group, and every Friday
   was a mess. Now guests show their code at the door, and we know who's coming before the
   night starts."
2. **Punchline:** "Guests show their code at the door, and we know who's coming before the
   night starts."
3. **For the venue onboarding page:** "Before this, Fridays were a mess of paper lists and
   WhatsApp. I'd recommend it to any bar."
