# Playbook: Freelance Rate, Scope Creep, and Deadline Pressure

Goal: quote a rate you can stand behind, and keep the scope tied to it. On
contract backend work the money is rarely lost in the first quote. It leaks
later, one "small" feature or one pulled-in deadline at a time.

## Sequence
1. **Get their numbers and constraints before giving yours.**
   > "What budget did you have in mind for this?"
   > "What happens on your side if this isn't live by then?"
   The deadline's real cost tells you how much the date is worth to them.
2. **Quote with an accusation audit, then a firm number.**
   > "You're probably going to think this is on the high side. For a payment
   > integration that has to be right the first time, it's [rate]."
   Then stop talking. Don't fill the silence with a discount.
3. **Label the "just one more thing."**
   > "It sounds like this turned out bigger than the original brief."
   Let them say yes. The label makes the creep visible without accusing anyone.
4. **Hand the trade-off back (calibrated question).**
   > "How would you like to handle it — move the date, or drop something from
   > this phase?"
   > "What should come out to make room for this?"
5. **Hold the "how" under deadline pressure.**
   > "How am I supposed to ship that by Friday and still have it tested?"
   A late, solid release beats an on-time incident. Say what you'd cut, not
   what you'd skip.
6. **Put the agreement in writing, warmly.**
   > "Let me write up what we agreed so neither of us has to remember it."

## Highest-leverage lines
- "What budget did you have in mind?" *(get their anchor first)*
- "It sounds like this grew past the original scope." *(label)*
- "What should come out to make room for this?" *(trade, don't absorb)*
- "How am I supposed to do that and still have it tested?" *(protect quality)*
- "Would it be a bad idea to put this in a phase two?" *(no-oriented)*

## Traps
- Don't absorb small additions for goodwill. They set the price of the next one.
- Don't drop the rate to win the job. Trade scope, timeline, or payment terms instead.
- Don't promise a date before you've seen the existing code.
- Don't argue about whether something is "in scope." Label it and ask how they want to handle it.
